General Discharge Guidelines and Post-Visit Care

These instructions supplement — they do not replace — the personalised discharge instructions provided by your care team at discharge. Always follow the specific instructions given to you by your physician, nurse, or care coordinator. If you did not receive personalised discharge instructions, contact your care team immediately.

SECTION 1: IMMEDIATELY AFTER YOUR VISIT OR PROCEDURE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1.1 Transportation
If you received sedation, anaesthesia, or any medication that may impair your reflexes or judgement:
   • You MUST have a responsible adult drive you home. Taxis and rideshare services are acceptable ONLY if a trusted adult accompanies you.
   • Do NOT drive or operate heavy machinery for at least 24 hours after receiving sedation or general anaesthesia.
   • Do NOT make important legal or financial decisions while under the influence of sedation medications.

1.2 Monitoring After Sedation
For the first 24 hours after receiving sedation:
   • Have a responsible adult with you at home.
   • Rest quietly and avoid strenuous activity.
   • Drink clear fluids; advance to a regular diet as tolerated.
   • Do not consume alcohol.

SECTION 2: GENERAL POST-VISIT ACTIVITY GUIDELINES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

2.1 Physical Activity
Unless your care team specified otherwise:
   • Rest for the remainder of the day of your visit.
   • Light activities (walking, light housework) may be resumed the next day if you feel well.
   • Avoid strenuous exercise, heavy lifting (> 10 lbs / 4.5 kg), or vigorous activity for 48 hours after an invasive procedure.
   • Swim, bathe in a tub, or submerge the procedure site only after your provider has confirmed wound healing.

2.2 Diet and Fluid Intake
   • Stay well hydrated: aim for 8–10 glasses of water per day unless fluid-restricted.
   • Eat light, easy-to-digest foods for the first 24 hours if nauseous (crackers, toast, soup, rice).
   • Avoid alcohol for 48 hours if you received sedation, antibiotics, or certain other medications.

2.3 Return to Work
Return-to-work timelines depend on your procedure and job requirements:
   • Sedation/minor procedures: typically 24–48 hours, depending on how you feel.
   • Invasive procedures/surgery: your provider will specify; typically 1–6 weeks.
   • If your job involves physical labour, discuss specific restrictions with your provider.

SECTION 3: MEDICATIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

3.1 New Prescriptions
   • Fill your new prescriptions at the pharmacy as soon as possible after discharge.
   • Read the medication guide provided with your prescription.
   • Take medications exactly as prescribed — do not double-dose if you miss a dose; follow the medication's specific instructions.

3.2 Over-the-Counter Pain Management
Unless you have been prescribed specific pain medication or have contraindications:
   • Mild to moderate pain: Acetaminophen (Tylenol) 500–1000 mg every 6–8 hours as needed (maximum: 4,000 mg in 24 hours).
   • Avoid ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin) or aspirin after invasive procedures unless your provider specifically approves, as they can increase bleeding risk.

3.3 Existing Medications
   • Resume all your regular medications unless your discharge instructions say otherwise.
   • If your provider changed a dosage or discontinued a medication, follow those instructions carefully.
   • Contact your provider if you are unsure whether to take a particular medication.

SECTION 4: WOUND AND PROCEDURE SITE CARE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

4.1 General Wound Care
   • Keep the wound site clean and dry for the first 24–48 hours.
   • Gently clean with mild soap and water as instructed; pat dry.
   • Apply prescribed ointments or dressings according to your specific instructions.
   • Change dressings as directed, or if they become wet, soiled, or loose.

4.2 Signs of Infection (Seek Medical Attention)
Contact your provider or go to urgent care if you notice:
   • Increasing redness, warmth, or swelling around the wound.
   • Purulent (pus-like) drainage or foul odour from the wound.
   • Red streaks extending from the wound.
   • Fever above 101°F (38.3°C).
   • Increasing pain not relieved by prescribed medication.

SECTION 5: FOLLOW-UP APPOINTMENTS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

5.1 Importance of Follow-Up
Follow-up appointments are a critical part of your care. They allow your provider to:
   • Review test results, pathology, or imaging.
   • Monitor healing and adjust your treatment plan.
   • Address any complications early.
   • Manage chronic conditions or newly diagnosed conditions.

5.2 Scheduling Your Follow-Up
Your provider recommended a follow-up appointment timeline. To schedule:
   • Patient portal: mypatientportal.healthorg.com
   • Phone: (555) 123-4567 | Monday–Friday, 7:00 AM – 7:00 PM
   • Do NOT wait until symptoms worsen to schedule your follow-up.

SECTION 6: WHEN TO SEEK IMMEDIATE MEDICAL ATTENTION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Call 911 or go to the nearest Emergency Room IMMEDIATELY if you experience:
   🚨 Chest pain, pressure, or tightness.
   🚨 Sudden difficulty breathing or shortness of breath.
   🚨 Sudden severe headache unlike any you've had before.
   🚨 Sudden weakness, numbness, or paralysis of face, arm, or leg.
   🚨 Sudden vision changes or slurred speech.
   🚨 Uncontrolled bleeding that does not stop with 10 minutes of direct pressure.
   🚨 Signs of severe allergic reaction: hives, throat swelling, difficulty breathing.
   🚨 Fever above 103°F (39.4°C).
   🚨 Altered mental status, confusion, or loss of consciousness.
   🚨 Severe abdominal pain or rigid abdomen.

For urgent but non-emergency concerns (fever 101–103°F, worsening pain not relieved by medication, inability to keep fluids down), call our Nurse Triage Line: (555) 123-4599 — available 24/7.
