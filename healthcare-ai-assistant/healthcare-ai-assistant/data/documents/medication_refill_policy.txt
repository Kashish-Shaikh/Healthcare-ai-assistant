SECTION 1: GENERAL REFILL GUIDELINES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1.1 Standard Refill Request Process
To request a medication refill, you may:
   Option A — Patient Portal: Log in → Medications → Request Refill. Select the medication and preferred pharmacy. Requests are processed within 2 business days.
   Option B — Pharmacy Direct: Most pharmacies can send electronic refill requests directly to your provider on your behalf. Allow 2–3 business days.
   Option C — Phone: Call your provider's office during business hours: Monday–Friday, 8:00 AM – 5:00 PM at (555) 234-5678.

1.2 Processing Time
Standard refill requests are processed within:
   • Routine medications (non-controlled): 2 business days.
   • Controlled substances (Schedule II–V): Up to 5 business days; may require an in-person or telehealth visit.
   • Prior authorisation required: 5–10 business days (depends on insurance response).

1.3 Best Practices
   • Request refills 7–10 days before your current supply runs out.
   • Keep your pharmacy contact information updated in the patient portal.
   • Ensure your insurance information is current to avoid prior authorisation delays.

SECTION 2: CONTROLLED SUBSTANCE PRESCRIPTIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

2.1 DEA Schedule Classifications
   • Schedule II:  High potential for abuse (e.g., oxycodone, Adderall, fentanyl).
   • Schedule III: Moderate potential (e.g., buprenorphine, certain testosterone preparations).
   • Schedule IV:  Lower potential (e.g., benzodiazepines, tramadol, zolpidem).
   • Schedule V:   Lowest potential (e.g., cough preparations with codeine).

2.2 Requirements for Controlled Substance Refills
Controlled substance prescriptions have special requirements:
   (a) Schedule II medications CANNOT be refilled — a new prescription must be issued each time.
   (b) Schedules III–V may be refilled up to 5 times within 6 months of the original prescription date.
   (c) A review visit (in-person or telehealth) is required at least every 90 days for Schedule II–III medications.
   (d) Urine drug screening may be required as part of your medication management programme.
   (e) Lost or stolen controlled substance prescriptions will not be replaced without a police report and provider review.

2.3 Pain Management Agreement
Patients prescribed opioid medications must sign a Pain Management Agreement, which includes:
   • Agreement to use medications only as prescribed.
   • Consent to random drug screening.
   • Agreement not to seek controlled substances from other providers simultaneously.
   • Acknowledgement of safe storage and disposal requirements.

SECTION 3: PRIOR AUTHORISATION FOR MEDICATIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

3.1 When Prior Authorisation Is Required
Insurance plans may require prior authorisation (PA) for:
   • Brand-name medications when a generic is available.
   • High-cost specialty medications.
   • Off-label medication use.
   • Certain quantities exceeding the plan's standard.
   • Step therapy requirements (trying lower-cost alternatives first).

3.2 PA Process
   (1) Your provider submits a PA request with clinical justification to your insurance.
   (2) Insurance reviews the request (standard: 3–5 business days; urgent: 24–72 hours).
   (3) Our Pharmacy Coordination Team notifies you of the decision.
   (4) If denied, you and your provider can file an appeal.

3.3 Bridge Supply
If you will run out of medication while awaiting PA approval, ask your provider about:
   • A bridge supply: A short-term prescription for a smaller quantity.
   • Manufacturer samples.
   • Patient assistance programmes (see Section 5).

SECTION 4: SPECIALTY MEDICATIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

4.1 Definition
Specialty medications are complex, high-cost drugs that often require special handling (refrigeration, injection training) and ongoing monitoring. Examples include biologics (adalimumab, etanercept), oncology agents, MS therapies, and HIV antiretrovirals.

4.2 Specialty Pharmacy
Most specialty medications are dispensed through our contracted specialty pharmacy:
   MedSpecialty Rx: 1-800-555-1234 | www.medspecialtyrx.com
   They provide: home delivery, injection training, refill reminders, and co-pay assistance.

4.3 Cold Chain Medications
Medications requiring refrigeration will be shipped with ice packs and temperature monitoring. Ensure someone is available to receive the delivery, or arrange for in-pharmacy pickup.

SECTION 5: PATIENT ASSISTANCE PROGRAMMES
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

If you are unable to afford your medications, our Pharmacy Benefits team can assist with:
   • Manufacturer Patient Assistance Programmes (PAP): Free or low-cost medications for eligible uninsured or underinsured patients.
   • GoodRx / NeedyMeds coupons: Significant discounts at retail pharmacies.
   • State Pharmaceutical Assistance Programmes (SPAP): Available in many states for seniors and low-income patients.
   • 340B Drug Pricing Programme: Discounted medications at our federally qualified health centre locations.

SECTION 6: MEDICATION DISPOSAL
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

6.1 Safe Disposal
Never flush medications down the toilet (except for a few FDA-authorised exceptions) or throw them in household trash. Options:
   • Drug Take-Back Days: Organised by the DEA twice yearly.
   • Year-round drop boxes: Available at all our clinic locations and many pharmacies.
   • FDA flush list: A small number of high-risk opioids may be flushed — check the FDA website.

6.2 At-Home Disposal (if no take-back option is available)
   (1) Mix medications with coffee grounds, dirt, or kitty litter in a sealed bag.
   (2) Remove personal information from labels.
   (3) Dispose of the sealed bag in household trash.

SECTION 7: MEDICATION INTERACTIONS AND SAFETY
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Always inform your provider and pharmacist of ALL medications you take, including:
   • Over-the-counter drugs (aspirin, ibuprofen, antacids, antihistamines).
   • Vitamins and dietary supplements.
   • Herbal remedies (St. John's Wort, ginkgo, valerian).
   • Recreational substances.
   This information prevents dangerous drug-drug or drug-supplement interactions.
